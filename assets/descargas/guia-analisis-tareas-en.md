# Analyse your tasks

*A guide to fostering students' digital competence and learning through what you ask them to do*

https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/recursos/

This guide helps you look at one specific task —an activity, an exercise, a project— and decide on **one improvement** that makes your students think, build and decide more, with or without technology.

You need about 20 minutes per task. It works better in pairs or with your school mentor. Fill it in by hand or edit the text version. Do not write any personal data about students.

## How to use it

- Choose a real task you will set soon, not your best one.
- Go through the eight steps in order; there is no need to write much: tick and jot down.
- Always finish with one single change, small and testable.
- If you want a second opinion, use the prompts at the end (or the website) with any AI and check what it suggests.

## Step 2. ICAP: what do students do with information?

Look at what students do, not the tool. Tick the main level.

- [ ] Passive: receives (watches, listens, reads)
- [ ] Active: manipulates (copies, underlines, completes, chooses)
- [ ] Constructive: generates something not in the material (explains, compares, creates)
- [ ] Interactive: co-constructs with someone else who also contributes

**Evidence: what would you see or hear in class that shows it?**

> 


> **Tip.** The biggest leap is usually from Active to Constructive. Question to climb one step: what will students produce that was not already in what I gave them?

## Step 5. The AI test

Paste the task (without personal data) into a generative AI and ask it to do the task as if it were a student. What happens?

- [ ] It solves it alone and well, in seconds
- [ ] It solves part of it
- [ ] It cannot: it needs students' own data, justified decisions or interaction with others

**Which part of the work do students still have to think through?**

> 


> **Tip.** It is not about banning AI but about making sure the task still requires thinking. Add something the AI does not have: data from your classroom, a decision to defend, a conversation or process evidence.

## Worked example

Original task (Year 8): «Write a 300-word essay on the importance of water».

- **ICAP:** Active, close to passive: it reorganises existing information.
- **PICRAT:** if handed in on a word processor, Passive-Replace: the medium changes, the thinking does not.
- **Seven keys:** building 0, process feedback 0, AI with friction 0.
- **AI test:** it solves it alone in seconds.
- **Digital competence:** none really; at most, writing in a document (shallow 6.3).
- **Change:** «Measure the water used at home for a week, compare your data with a classmate's and propose a measure to the school based on them. AI may help you analyse the data, but the data and the decision are yours».
- **Now students have to:** collect data, compare it, interpret differences, decide and defend a proposal (Constructive and Interactive; 6.1 and 6.5).
- **Evidence:** data sheet, the pair's decision log and a two-minute defence.

## Continue with AI: linked prompts

Copy the prompt from the website (it has drop-downs and buttons to open it in Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude) or from the prompt workbook. Follow the order: each one leads to the next.

1. **X-ray of a task**: Analyse one of your tasks with ICAP, PICRAT, the seven keys, the AI test and students’ digital competence, and come away with two concrete improvements. https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#analizar-tarea
2. **Climb one ICAP step**: Redesign an activity so students move from receiving or manipulating to generating or co-constructing, without changing the goal. https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#analizar-icap
3. **Place your task in PICRAT**: See clearly who does the intellectual work with technology and design the next move on the matrix. https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#analizar-picrat
4. **Which digital competence do your students develop?**: Check which digital competences students really develop (not which tools they use) and make them explicit with an instruction. https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#analizar-cd-alumnado
5. **From copyable to knowledge-generating**: Redesign a task so it requires thinking an AI cannot replace. https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#resistentes-redisenar
6. **Assess the process, not just the product**: Design process evidence and a rubric that values the journey, so learning is visible even when AI exists. https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#evaluar-proceso
7. **Exemplars and peer review**: Teach students to judge quality and use feedback: invented exemplars, peer review and a response log. https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#evaluar-ejemplares
8. **A lesson with productive friction**: Plan an evidence-based lesson: phases with and without screens, a constructive and an interactive phase, an attempt before help and a metacognitive close. https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#planificar-secuencia
9. **Reflective practice journal**: After a lesson, think about what happened with a mentor who asks rather than judges, and decide on a single adjustment. https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#planificar-reflexion
