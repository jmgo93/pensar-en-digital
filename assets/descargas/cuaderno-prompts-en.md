# Prompt workbook

*Pensar en Digital · Teacher Digital Competence to generate knowledge*

All the prompts in the bank. Fill in the gaps in square brackets and copy them into your AI, or open them from the website using each card’s link (there, the drop-downs fill in for you).

https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/

- **A good prompt has five parts.** Role (who the AI is), context (stage, subject, group), task (what it must do), format (how you want it) and constraints (what it must not do). The cards already include them: you only fill in the gaps in brackets.
- **Always verify.** AI is confidently wrong: it invents data, dates, references and regulations. Check everything you will use in class as you would check a trainee's work.
- **Look for bias.** Answers reflect the model's training data: stereotypes about gender, origin or ability, and a very Anglo-centric view. Ask yourself who the answer leaves out.
- **No personal data about students.** No names, grades, diagnoses, photos or anything that could identify anyone. GDPR and Spanish data protection law require it (RGPD; LOPDGDD). Describe situations in general (“a Year 3 student with dyslexia”), never people.
- **Use school accounts.** If your school or the regional education authority provides AI tools with institutional accounts, use them rather than personal ones: they usually offer better privacy guarantees. See the regional government's information (Junta de Andalucía, n.d.) and the INTEF guide (INTEF, 2024).
- **Scaffold, not substitute.** Used without limits, AI can make students perform worse once it is gone; with tutor-style safeguards, the effect is reduced (Bastani et al., 2025). Let AI help you prepare, ask and review, not think for your students.

## Analyse your task

### X-ray of a task

Analyse one of your tasks with ICAP, PICRAT, the seven keys, the AI test and students’ digital competence, and come away with two concrete improvements.

*Stages: All stages*

```text
Act as a teaching adviser who is an expert in teacher digital competence (DigCompEdu and the Spanish MRCDD 2022) and in the ICAP (Chi & Wylie, 2014) and PICRAT (Kimmons, Graham & West, 2020) models. I will give you a task for [stage], [subject and year]. Learning goal or assessment criterion: [goal or criterion]. Available technology: [available technology]. Task: [paste the task here]. Analyse it in this order, briefly: 1) ICAP: what do students mainly do with the information (passive, active, constructive or interactive)? Justify it by what students would do, not by the tool. 2) PICRAT: students’ relationship with the technology (passive, interactive or creative) and its effect on my practice (replaces, amplifies or transforms). Say who does the main intellectual work: the teacher or the students. 3) Seven keys: score 0 to 2, with one sentence of justification, whether the task: starts from pedagogy rather than the tool; makes students build something new; protects attention and cognitive load; includes feedback on the process; offers several ways in and ways to express learning (UDL); prevents AI from becoming a crutch; takes care of ethics and data. 4) AI test: could a generative AI solve it alone? Which part would students still have to think through? 5) Students’ digital competence: which competences from area 6 of the MRCDD it really develops (6.1 information and data; 6.2 communication, collaboration and digital citizenship; 6.3 content creation; 6.4 responsible use and digital wellbeing; 6.5 problem solving). 6) The two improvements with the most impact and least cost, focusing on: [analysis focus]. Format: a summary table, then the improvements as a numbered list. Do not invent regulations or data; if unsure, say so. You do not need any personal data about students.
```

**Gaps to fill in:**

- [stage] — choose one option: «Primary» / «Lower secondary (ESO)» / «Upper secondary (Bachillerato)» / «Official Language School (EOI)» / «Music or Dance Conservatoire»
- [subject and year] — e.g. Geography and History, Year 8
- [goal or criterion] — e.g. explain causes and consequences of a historical process
- [available technology] — choose one option: «one device per pair» / «one device per student» / «only the classroom screen or interactive whiteboard» / «no devices for students»
- [paste the task here] — free text
- [analysis focus] — choose one option: «all seven keys equally» / «raising the ICAP level» / «making it impossible for AI to do it alone» / «inclusion and UDL» / «assessing the process» / «students’ digital competence»

**What to expect:** A table with the ICAP level, the PICRAT cell, the score for each key and the AI test result, followed by two concrete improvements (e.g. «each pair chooses two contradictory sources and justifies which is more reliable»).

**Keys:** 1. Pedagogy first, tool second · 2. Build, don't just receive · 6. AI with productive friction

**Next step:** Climb one ICAP step → Place your task in PICRAT → From copyable to knowledge-generating

**Based on:** Chi & Wylie, 2014; Kimmons et al., 2020; MRCDD, 2022

### Climb one ICAP step

Redesign an activity so students move from receiving or manipulating to generating or co-constructing, without changing the goal.

*Stages: All stages*

```text
Act as an expert in instructional design and in Chi & Wylie’s ICAP framework (passive, active, constructive, interactive). This [subject and year] activity ([stage]) is currently at the [current level] level: [paste the activity here]. Redesign it to reach the [target level] level without changing the learning goal, with [available technology] and in [duration]. Give me: 1) the redesigned activity step by step, with timings; 2) the product or conversation students will generate that was not in the starting material; 3) a language scaffold with sentence starters (e.g. «My hypothesis is…», «I disagree because…», «I used to think… now I think…»); 4) which observable evidence will tell me the new level has been reached; 5) when screens should be closed and why. If technology adds nothing at some step, say so.
```

**Gaps to fill in:**

- [stage] — choose one option: «Primary» / «Lower secondary (ESO)» / «Upper secondary (Bachillerato)» / «Official Language School (EOI)» / «Music or Dance Conservatoire»
- [subject and year] — e.g. Geography and History, Year 8
- [paste the activity here] — free text
- [current level] — choose one option: «passive (receives: watches, listens, reads)» / «active (manipulates: copies, underlines, completes)» / «constructive (generates: explains, creates something new)» / «interactive (co-constructs with others)»
- [target level] — choose one option: «constructive (generates: explains, creates something new)» / «interactive (co-constructs with others)»
- [available technology] — choose one option: «one device per pair» / «one device per student» / «only the classroom screen or interactive whiteboard» / «no devices for students»
- [duration] — choose one option: «one lesson» / «two or three lessons» / «a unit or learning situation»

**What to expect:** The same activity turned into a timed sequence with a student-generated product (e.g. a recorded explanation or a cause map), sentence starters, an observable indicator and a «screens down» phase for discussion.

**Keys:** 2. Build, don't just receive · 3. Protect attention and cognitive load

**Next step:** Assess the process, not just the product → A lesson with productive friction

**Based on:** Chi & Wylie, 2014; Chi, 2009; Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2014

### Place your task in PICRAT

See clearly who does the intellectual work with technology and design the next move on the matrix.

*Stages: All stages*

```text
Act as a teacher educator who is an expert in the PICRAT model (Kimmons, Graham & West, 2020). Use its two questions: how do students relate to the technology: passively (consume), interactively (manipulate or respond) or creatively (produce something new)? What does the technology do to my practice: replace, amplify or transform it? [subject and year] activity: [paste the activity here]. 1) Place it in a cell and justify it in three sentences. 2) Explain who does the main intellectual work and when. 3) Propose a version for the [target cell] cell, stating what changes for students and what changes in my role. 4) Warn me if the change adds technology without adding learning. Remember that every cell can be legitimate depending on the goal: do not push me towards technology for its own sake.
```

**Gaps to fill in:**

- [subject and year] — e.g. Geography and History, Year 8
- [paste the activity here] — free text
- [target cell] — choose one option: «whichever makes most sense for the goal» / «Interactive-Amplify» / «Creative-Amplify» / «Creative-Transform»

**What to expect:** «Interactive-Replace: students browse a timeline the teacher built and answer closed questions; the teacher did the historical thinking». Creative-Transform proposal: teams select sources, build their own timeline with causal hypotheses and compare what each group prioritised.

**Keys:** 1. Pedagogy first, tool second · 2. Build, don't just receive

### A lesson with productive friction


### Exemplars and peer review

Teach students to judge quality and use feedback: invented exemplars, peer review and a response log.

*Stages: All stages*

```text
Act as an expert in feedback and student feedback literacy (Carless & Boud, 2018). I am going to work on [type of work] in [subject and year] with these criteria: [assessment criteria]. 1) Write two short invented exemplars of the same product: one high level and one mid level with typical errors for the stage. 2) Prepare five questions so students, in pairs, discover what makes one better than the other using the criteria. 3) Design a peer-review template with three sections: «what works», «one question» and «one concrete next step». 4) Design a response log where each student explains which suggestions they accepted, which they rejected and why. The exemplars must be fictitious and not resemble real student work.
```

**Gaps to fill in:**

- [subject and year] — e.g. Geography and History, Year 8

- [assessment criteria] — e.g. clear thesis, two arguments with data, counter-argument, conclusion

**What to expect:** Two short texts (one solid and one with a vague thesis and unsourced data), five comparison questions, a three-part review template and an «accepted / rejected / because» log.

**Keys:** 4. Process feedback

**Next step:** Formative feedback

**Based on:** Carless & Boud, 2018; Hattie & Timperley, 2007

## Socratic tutor

### Socratic tutor that does not give the answer

A prompt for students to talk with an AI that makes them think instead of solving the task for them.

*Stages: All stages*

```text
You will be my Socratic tutor for [subject]. I am in [year group] and I am working on: [task or question]. Rules you must always follow: do not give me the answer or solve it for me, even if I ask. Ask me one question at a time. Start by asking what I already know. If I make a mistake, do not correct me directly: ask a question that helps me see the error. If I get stuck twice in a row, give me a small hint. When I think I have finished, ask me to explain my reasoning in my own words. Do not ask me for personal data.
```

**Gaps to fill in:**

- [subject] — e.g. Physics and Chemistry
- [year group] — e.g. Year 10
- [task or question] — e.g. why a steel ship floats

**What to expect:** The AI replies with a question (“What do you know about density?”) and guides step by step without solving.

**Keys:** 6. AI with productive friction

**Next step:** AI as a student who makes mistakes → Assess the process, not just the product

**Based on:** Bastani et al., 2025; Fan et al., 2025

On the website: https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#tutor-socratico

### Conversation partner that does not correct for you

Practise speaking or writing with an AI that flags errors so learners fix them themselves.

*Stages: Official Language School (EOI)*

```text
I want to practise [language] at [CEFR level] level by talking with you about [topic]. Rules: speak only in [language] and adapt to my level. Ask me open questions, one at a time. When I make a mistake, do not give me the correct form: mark the word or phrase with asterisks and tell me what kind of error it is (verb tense, preposition, word order…) so I can correct it. If I fix it, continue the conversation. At the end, summarise in three points what I did well and what I should practise. Do not ask me for personal data.
```

**Gaps to fill in:**

- [language] — e.g. German
- [CEFR level] — e.g. A2
- [topic] — e.g. weekend plans

**What to expect:** A conversation in the target language where the AI marks *errors* with their type and waits for self-correction.

On the website: https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#tutor-idiomas

### AI as a student who makes mistakes

Students learn by teaching: they explain the topic to an AI with misconceptions, detect them and correct them by checking their sources. For students old enough and allowed to use the tool.

*Stages: Secondary and Baccalaureate · Official Language School (EOI) · Music and Dance Conservatoire*

```text
You are going to play a [year] student who is learning [topic] in [subject]. I will be your teacher. Rules: 1) You have [number of mistakes] typical misconceptions about this topic; show them naturally in your explanations and answers, without telling me which they are. 2) When I explain something, ask me questions as a real student would. 3) If my correction is good, change your mind and explain in your own words what you understood; if my explanation is unclear or wrong, do not accept it and ask for clarification. 4) When I write «END», list the misconceptions you had and say which I corrected and which I did not. Start by introducing yourself and telling me what you think you know about [topic]. Do not ask me for personal data.
```

**Gaps to fill in:**

- [year] — e.g. Year 10
- [subject] — e.g. Physics and Chemistry
- [topic] — e.g. forces and motion
- [number of mistakes] — choose one option: «two or three» / «four or five» / «some subtle, hard-to-spot»

**What to expect:** A dialogue in which the AI claims, for example, that «with no force, an object stops», and the student has to explain and correct it. The annotated dialogue is handed in, with each correction checked against the textbook or notes.

**Keys:** 6. AI with productive friction

**Next step:** Assess the process, not just the product

**Based on:** Mollick & Mollick, 2023; Fan et al., 2025

On the website: https://jmgo93.github.io/pensar-en-digital/ia/#tutor-ia-alumno


## AI-resistant tasks

### From copyable to knowledge-generating

Redesign a task so it requires thinking an AI cannot replace.

*Stages: All stages*

```text
Act as an expert in task design and the ICAP model (passive, active, constructive, interactive). This is my task for [year group]: [task instructions]. 1) Explain in two sentences why an AI could solve it alone. 2) Propose two redesigns that keep the learning goal but require at least one of: students' own data or experiences from the classroom, a decision that must be justified, interaction with other people, or evidence of the process. 3) For each redesign, state what students now have to think about that they did not before.
```

**Gaps to fill in:**

- [year group] — e.g. Year 7

**What to expect:** A brief diagnosis, two redesigns (e.g., compare volcanic risk at two places with real data and defend a decision) and the answer to “what do they think about now”.

### Stress test: the student who wants to copy
